Elevation sensitivity: a necessary robustness check

An independently successful ERA5 geopotential × IEM station-elevation audit found that ABQ sits approximately 155 m below its bilinearly interpolated ERA5 surface elevation, versus 41 m below at ROW. A common illustrative 6.5 K/km adjustment raises all grid predictions by 1.008 K at ABQ and 0.265 K at ROW.

Under this assumption, the source-mean minus historical annual RMSE contrast switches sign in two of three ABQ seeds, but none of the ROW seeds. This illustrates why apparent hundredth-kelvin channel differences should not be presented as a robust scientific improvement. The associated 14-day paired bootstrap shows that all three ABQ seed intervals include zero after 6.5 K/km adjustment. ROW seed 42 retains a positive interval (+0.00514 to +0.02391 K), while ROW seed 7 has a negative interval (−0.01341 to −0.00109 K), underscoring cross-seed disagreement. The analysis also archives 0, 3.0, and 9.8 K/km scenarios; it does not estimate a time-varying atmospheric lapse rate or calibrate a forecast model. Review the 24-row sensitivity audit.

Cross-case extension (new): The same independently audited station elevations have now been applied to the accepted Southwest 2023 and Southern Plains 2022 ASOS comparisons. At an illustrative 6.5 K/km lapse rate, Albuquerque seed 123 and Roswell seed 7 reverse their source-channel RMSE ranking, while the Abilene and Oklahoma City rankings do not reverse. This extends the representativeness audit to four site-years without retraining the archived models. The 12 paired results are publicly documented; the larger 144-scenario CI replay remains subject to its separate GitHub Actions acceptance gate.

The observed-hot ≥305 K subset includes 42 ABQ records and 180 ROW records; the channel ranking changes across model seeds, especially at ROW. This is a retrospective station-observation consistency check, not independent operational 24-hour forecast validation or heat-warning accuracy. Inspect exact metrics and CI bounds.

Engineering outcomes

Real NVIDIA Tesla T4 · Google Colab

7.76× observed training-time ratio

Initial matched-configuration Colab CPU training took 258.51 s and GPU 33.29 s; three additional audited GPU processes averaged 33.45 s (sample SD 1.92 s). This is a descriptive single-CPU-reference comparison, not a paired multi-run speedup benchmark.

Reproducibility · Hardware audit

CPU/GPU RMSE parity

Both paths produced 3.191155 K RMSE on the same 2022 test, with identical input SHA-256, matched settings and verified GPU-resident parameters. Three repeated GPU runs reproduced the same test RMSE.

Scientific methodology

Forecasting ERA5 six-hourly 2 m temperature at a 24-hour lead, using four prior frames and known forecast-time features. Data are split chronologically: training-only month/UTC-hour climatology, validation-only blend and model selection, followed by year-specific tests. The CNN predicts a residual correction to a strong persistence–climatology mixture. A 305 K fixed-temperature training-loss emphasis was assessed over three random seeds.

Multiday heat episode analysis

Events are defined retrospectively by observed temperature: at least 5% of cells reach ≥305 K at one or more six-hourly valid times per day for at least two consecutive days. Compared with the blend, the three seed-2-weight CNN runs improved event-conditioned hot RMSE on 7/7 episodes each in the 2021 retrospective window, 8/8 each in the original-region 2022 test, and 8/10, 10/10 and 10/10 episodes in the Southwest-region 2022 test. This does not evaluate whether the model detects or warns about extreme-heat events.

What this evidence does—and does not—show

Demonstrated: reproducible real ERA5 engineering; actual CUDA-backed JAX training and CPU/GPU accuracy parity; stronger baseline comparisons; multiple model seeds; retrospective regional and event-conditioned diagnostics. Not demonstrated: a reliable annual forecasting advantage in every seed, frozen-weights zero-shot spatial transfer, operational forecast skill, statistically independent events, or untouched prospective external evaluation. The 2021 backtest involved a period used earlier in model development, and the second geographic experiment retrained model weights locally.
