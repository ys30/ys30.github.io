Track 01 · Systems and model implementation

Engineering Results — JAX and GPU

This section reports the earlier Colab CPU/Tesla T4 engineering benchmark and separately evaluated heat-weighted regional CNN experiments. They are not the later frozen-weight factorial study.

Real NVIDIA Tesla T4 · Google Colab

7.76× observed training-time ratio

Initial matched-configuration Colab CPU training took 258.51 s and GPU 33.29 s; three additional audited GPU processes averaged 33.45 s (sample SD 1.92 s). This is a descriptive single-CPU-reference comparison, not a paired multi-run speedup benchmark.

Reproducibility · Hardware audit

CPU/GPU RMSE parity

Both paths produced 3.191155 K RMSE on the same 2022 test, with identical input SHA-256, matched settings and verified GPU-resident parameters. Three repeated GPU runs reproduced the same test RMSE.

Engineering workflow and model design

Forecasting ERA5 six-hourly 2 m temperature at a 24-hour lead, using four prior frames and known forecast-time features. Data are split chronologically: training-only month/UTC-hour climatology, validation-only blend and model selection, followed by year-specific tests. The CNN predicts a residual correction to a strong persistence–climatology mixture. A 305 K fixed-temperature training-loss emphasis was assessed over three random seeds.

Multiday heat episode analysis

Events are defined retrospectively by observed temperature: at least 5% of cells reach ≥305 K at one or more six-hourly valid times per day for at least two consecutive days. Compared with the blend, the three seed-2-weight CNN runs improved event-conditioned hot RMSE on 7/7 episodes each in the 2021 retrospective window, 8/8 each in the original-region 2022 test, and 8/10, 10/10 and 10/10 episodes in the Southwest-region 2022 test. This does not evaluate whether the model detects or warns about extreme-heat events.

Engineering limits and scope

Demonstrated: reproducible real ERA5 engineering; actual CUDA-backed JAX training and CPU/GPU accuracy parity; stronger baseline comparisons; multiple model seeds; retrospective regional and event-conditioned diagnostics. Not demonstrated: a reliable annual forecasting advantage in every seed, frozen-weights zero-shot spatial transfer, operational forecast skill, statistically independent events, or untouched prospective external evaluation. The 2021 backtest involved a period used earlier in model development, and the second geographic experiment retrained model weights locally.

Track 02 · Working research manuscript

Research / Paper Results — Where climatology enters matters

Research question: Does the incremental value of an explicit historical climatology input depend on whether the CNN's residual reference already includes climatology? Unlike the GPU engineering benchmark, this study uses independently trained, source-trained CNN arms evaluated with frozen weights in four held-out region–years. The working manuscript targets Atmospheric Research; it is not yet published or peer reviewed.

Controlled design

2 × 2 factorial, 10 seeds

Reference: 24-hour persistence (P) or validation-selected blend (B). Dedicated channel: source-mean (S) or pre-test historical climatology (H). Same seed across four fitted arms; no target-year model retraining.

Verification

Two regions · two years

Southwest and Southern Plains in 2022/2023, 1,460 six-hourly steps per case; 25 × 25 ERA5 grid. Full-year RMSE and observed-hot (verifying T2m ≥305 K).

Primary finding: the benefit of the extra climatology channel depends on the reference

Define ΔP = RMSE(P,S) − RMSE(P,H), ΔB = RMSE(B,S) − RMSE(B,H), and interaction I = ΔB − ΔP. Negative I means the historical input offers less extra benefit when the blend already carries climatological information.

The interaction is negative for 39/40 seed–case evaluations on all valid times and 30/40 on the truth-hot subset. These are repeated measurements using shared seed keys and source models—not 40 independent climate samples. The result is compatible with overlapping information, not proof of a unique causal/physical mechanism.

Independent observational context — ASOS

Four preselected airports (ABQ, ROW, ABI and OKC) were matched to 2022–2023 measurements (eight station-years). The historical-channel CNN beat its shared blended reference at station-observed hot times in 80/80 seed–station-year point comparisons, yet the concurrent ERA5 analysis matched stations better. ERA5 analysis is not an available 24-hour-ahead forecast. Annual station comparisons were less uniform, and separate six-station-year/three-seed audits had 9/18 positive channel differences.

Station agreement is a retrospective point-versus-grid check, not fully assimilation-independent operational verification. Spatial maps and station-bias decomposition remain supporting diagnostics; the primary paper evidence is the factorial contrast above.
