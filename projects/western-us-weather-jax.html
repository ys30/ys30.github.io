Track 01 · Systems and model implementation

Engineering Results — JAX and GPU

This section reports the earlier Colab CPU/Tesla T4 engineering benchmark and separately evaluated heat-weighted regional CNN experiments. They are not the later frozen-weight factorial study.

Real NVIDIA Tesla T4 · Google Colab

7.76× observed training-time ratio

Initial matched-configuration Colab CPU training took 258.51 s and GPU 33.29 s; three additional audited GPU processes averaged 33.45 s (sample SD 1.92 s). This is a descriptive single-CPU-reference comparison, not a paired multi-run speedup benchmark.

Reproducibility · Hardware audit

CPU/GPU RMSE parity

Both paths produced 3.191155 K RMSE on the same 2022 test, with identical input SHA-256, matched settings and verified GPU-resident parameters. Three repeated GPU runs reproduced the same test RMSE.

Engineering workflow and model design

Forecasting ERA5 six-hourly 2 m temperature at a 24-hour lead, using four prior frames and known forecast-time features. Data are split chronologically: training-only month/UTC-hour climatology, validation-only blend and model selection, followed by year-specific tests. The CNN predicts a residual correction to a strong persistence–climatology mixture. A 305 K fixed-temperature training-loss emphasis was assessed over three random seeds.

Separate engineering experiment: local model retraining

All reported values are retrospective 2022 test RMSE in kelvin; lower is better. Each regional model was retrained in its own region.

Multiday heat episode analysis

Events are defined retrospectively by observed temperature: at least 5% of cells reach ≥305 K at one or more six-hourly valid times per day for at least two consecutive days. Compared with the blend, the three seed-2-weight CNN runs improved event-conditioned hot RMSE on 7/7 episodes each in the 2021 retrospective window, 8/8 each in the original-region 2022 test, and 8/10, 10/10 and 10/10 episodes in the Southwest-region 2022 test. This does not evaluate whether the model detects or warns about extreme-heat events.

Engineering limits and scope

Demonstrated: reproducible real ERA5 engineering; actual CUDA-backed JAX training and CPU/GPU accuracy parity; stronger baseline comparisons; multiple model seeds; retrospective regional and event-conditioned diagnostics. Not demonstrated: a reliable annual forecasting advantage in every seed, frozen-weights zero-shot spatial transfer, operational forecast skill, statistically independent events, or untouched prospective external evaluation. The 2021 backtest involved a period used earlier in model development, and the second geographic experiment retrained model weights locally.

Track 02 · Research manuscript v4.5.3 · Atmospheric Research target

Research Results — Where climatology enters matters

The scientific question is not simply whether climatology helps. It is whether the value of a dedicated historical-climatology channel changes when a residual neural model already incorporates climatology in its reference forecast.

Status: manuscript prepared for submission to Atmospheric Research; not yet peer-reviewed or published. Fixed, publicly available v4.5.3 research code release ↗. Large model outputs are being prepared for permanent Zenodo publication; no DOI is claimed until published.

Matched factorial design

2 × 2 · 10 seeds · 4 cases

Source ERA5 training 2019–2020, source validation 2021; four individually trained arms, then frozen evaluation in Southwest and Southern Plains, 2022 and 2023. Each target case has 1,460 six-hourly times × 25 × 25 grid points.

Two climatology pathways

Reference × dedicated channel

Residual reference: P, 24-h persistence; or B, a validation-selected persistence–climatology blend (w = 0.25). Channel: S, source-mean constant; or H, target pre-test historical climatology.

Finding 1 · The same information has different marginal value depending on where it enters

Compared with persistence, a dedicated historical channel usually reduces RMSE more. When climatology is already included in the blended reference, its additional value is much smaller. The experiment contrasts ΔP = RMSE(P,S) − RMSE(P,H) with ΔB = RMSE(B,S) − RMSE(B,H); a negative I = ΔB − ΔP denotes a smaller incremental channel benefit with blend.

Full-year interactions are more consistent than those on cells selected by verifying ERA5 temperature ≥305 K. This pattern supports overlapping predictive information, but changing the residual reference also changes the target of optimization: the experiment does not uniquely identify the underlying mechanism.

Finding 2 · Model ranking depends on the region and the verification population

Across the four target cases, the blend + historical CNN usually gives the lowest mean observed-hot CNN RMSE, but Southern Plains 2022 is an exception. More importantly, the best incremental channel configuration and the best absolute forecast should not be conflated.

Finding 3 · A heat-conditioned conclusion depends on what was known when the forecast was issued

The v4.5.3 supplementary analysis asked why reference weights appear less favorable on retrospectively hot cases. It repeated the reference-only diagnostics on a forecast-time-known subset: grid cells with temperature at the preceding day, T(t−4), ≥305 K. No CNN was retrained for this analysis.

Across the four target cases, the no-intercept 24-h anomaly-persistence slope is 0.579–0.624 for all times but 0.754–0.840 in the predictor-hot subset. The grouped-mean display makes this behavior interpretable without unstable slopes near a zero anomaly.

The smaller predictor-hot diagnostic weight does not mean that the original blend performed worse than persistence. With lagged T ≥305 K, the original w = 0.25 blend actually reduced RMSE by 0.075–0.241 K in all four targets. By contrast, conditioning on the verifying temperature can change the reference ranking. External check · Airport-station observations

Four selected ASOS airports (ABQ, ROW, ABI and OKC) provide eight station-years in 2022–2023. The CNN improved on the blended reference at station-observed hot times in 80/80 seed–station-year comparisons, but the concurrent ERA5 analysis agreed better with stations. ERA5 analysis is a valid-time comparator, not a forecast available 24 h earlier.
