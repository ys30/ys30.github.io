When simpler models outperform complex ML

I built a spatially robust species-distribution modeling benchmark to answer a practical question: which predictions remain useful when evaluation geography, model complexity, and field-survey budgets change?

Key result: A well-specified, interpretable baseline often outperformed more complex alternatives. Geographic validation design and metric choice could change model rankings. These are retrospective within-region findings, not external deployment claims.

Three results worth remembering

01 · Complexity is not a free upgrade

Industry lesson: benchmark advanced ML against strong, interpretable baselines—not just a linear straw man.

02 · Spatial validation changes the answer

Industry lesson: evaluate where a model will actually be used; geographic leakage can distort comparisons.

03 · Good AUC is not the whole decision

The same survey budget recovered 17/18 recorded detections for one rare taxon, but only 3/8 for another. Historical retrospective retrieval, not a prospective field result.

04 · Hyperparameter tuning needs honest evaluation

Exploratory nested spatial tuning narrowed the difference between pooled and taxon-specific XGBoost, but tuned XGBoost did not overtake the quadratic logistic baseline on the reported within-taxon AUC comparisons.

The newer v6.25b tuning results are pre-submission and still awaiting a fresh independently verified public CI replay. No unpublished figure is presented as a verified public artifact.

Engineering principle: fit, tune and evaluate within correctly separated spatial data.

How the evaluation works

1. Survey data1,167 plots, eight taxa and environmental covariates
2. Spatial holdoutsGeographic blocks / clusters; sensitivity to distance buffers
3. Model comparisonsLogistic GLM, XGBoost, MaxEnt and Bayesian models
4. Decision metricsWithin-taxon AUC, log loss, calibration and top-k retrieval

Not every historical pipeline uses identical inputs or tuning protocols; matched comparisons are interpreted separately from broader sensitivity analyses. The nested-tuning extension was exploratory and is not described as a prespecified confirmatory experiment.

Technical deep dive & source evidence

Historical matched model comparison

The original matched three-pipeline analysis evaluated 23 eligible taxon–fold cells across five taxa: pooled XGBoost AUC 0.8016, MaxEnt 0.7865, hierarchical Bayesian 0.7610. Different feature sets mean this is a pipeline comparison, not an isolated algorithm effect.

Rare-taxon decision support

At 25 plots per fold, 125/1,167 plots (10.7%) were selected in total. Bayesian retrospective detection recovery was 3/8 (nsw19), 17/18 (nsw20), and 4/6 (nsw22). Observed non-detections are not detection-corrected absences.

Spatial uncertainty, calibration and limitations

Second-partition and buffered sensitivity analyses compared model discrimination, proper probability scores and residual spatial autocorrelation. Eight-taxon bootstrap intervals are exploratory, and some rare taxon–fold cells do not support defined AUC. Independent-region validation, prospective field outcomes, and full v6.25b CI reproduction remain open.
