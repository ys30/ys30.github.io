Ecological Species Distribution Modeling

How do three ecological prediction pipelines compare on identical spatially held-out plots, and can their rankings help prioritize surveys for sparsely detected taxa?

View code & full resultsAll projects

Project overview

Built a reproducible ecological classification workflow from 9,336 labeled species–plot records representing eight native tree species and 1,167 forest plots in New South Wales, Australia. Combined environmental variables, species identity and (in the plot-holdout experiment) geographic coordinates, and assessed performance with ROC AUC, average precision, calibration, and spatially explicit error diagnostics.

Current research benchmark: three matched SDM pipelines

Using the public disdat New South Wales open-forest benchmark, I evaluated 1,167 plots, eight anonymized taxa and 9,336 plot–taxon records. A five-fold geographic holdout produced 23 evaluable taxon–fold comparisons across five taxa. The models were assessed on the same held-out records but use different predictors and modeling assumptions.

PipelineMean ROC AUCModeling approach
Pooled XGBoost0.8016Boosted trees with taxon and environmental information
Species-specific MaxEnt0.7865Regularized environmental suitability
Hierarchical Bayesian0.7610Taxon-level partial pooling

The paired XGBoost–MaxEnt mean AUC difference was +0.0151. This is a descriptive comparison of implemented pipelines, not a controlled test of which algorithm is universally superior. The fixed-round XGBoost benchmark was not selected using nested spatial tuning.

Feature sensitivity: A fold-local encoding check gave similar full-feature discrimination. Removing species identity from pooled XGBoost changed the information set and substantially reduced performance; this is not solely an encoding effect.

Rare-taxon survey screening: At a budget of 25 held-out plots per fold, retrospective Bayesian rankings recovered 3/8, 17/18 and 4/6 recorded detections for three sparsely observed taxa. This is not prospective field-validation evidence.

Geographic scope: The spatial folds were not distance-buffered; 218 of 1,167 test plots were within 5 km of a training plot. The results support within-region holdout assessment, not demonstrated transfer to an independent landscape.

What the results mean

With only approximately 8% positive records, a high ROC AUC does not guarantee good detection of occurrences. At the default 0.50 threshold, the plot-grouped model recovered 36.9% of observed presences. An exploratory threshold sweep illustrates the precision–recall trade-off, but does not independently validate an optimized decision threshold.

Methods and limitations

R, XGBoost, environmental feature processing, plot-grouped evaluation, longitude-band and two-dimensional quantile-grid cross-validation, class-imbalance diagnostics, probability calibration, ggplot2, and automated GitHub Actions. Spatial blocks are not distance-buffered, and no independent external geographic test set is claimed. All maps display sampled coordinates rather than continuous predictions across unsampled territory.
