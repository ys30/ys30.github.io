Species Distribution Modeling in New South Wales

Comparing XGBoost, MaxEnt and hierarchical Bayesian models on the same geographically held-out survey records—and translating predictions into practical rare-taxon survey priorities.

Code & verified resultsAll projects

At a glance

1,167 forest plots · 8 anonymized taxa · 9,336 plot–taxon observations · 5 geographic folds · 23 matched taxon–fold evaluations

1. Where and how were models validated?

Public disdat NSW open-forest survey data. Each plot and all its taxon records stay together in one held-out fold. A two-dimensional 4×4 quantile grid assigns entire cells to five folds. The map uses actual survey coordinates and geographic boundaries.

2. What did the matched comparison find?

The same 23 eligible taxon–fold test records were used to compare three implemented pipelines across five evaluable taxa.

Model pipelineMean ROC AUCKey distinction
Pooled XGBoost0.8016Boosted trees; environmental and taxon-identity inputs
Species-specific MaxEnt0.7865Regularized environmental suitability
Hierarchical Bayesian0.7610Taxon-level partial pooling

Key result: XGBoost exceeded MaxEnt by a mean paired AUC of +0.0151. This is a modest descriptive advantage, not proof that boosted trees outperform other SDM algorithms in general: the pipelines use different predictor information and score interpretations.

3. What changes the interpretation?

Feature representation: Fold-local categorical encoding produced similar full-feature XGBoost discrimination. Removing taxon identity sharply reduced pooled-model performance, but also changed the prediction task; it cannot be interpreted as a pure encoding effect.

Model selection: The archived spatial XGBoost benchmark used a fixed 300-round configuration, not nested spatial hyperparameter tuning. Bayesian convergence checks passed in independent longer-chain fits, while the matched comparison uses archived original-fit predictions.

4. Can the models guide a limited survey budget?

In retrospective screening of the three sparse taxa, selecting the top 25 held-out plots per fold recovered 3/8, 17/18 and 4/6 recorded detections, respectively. Performance is highly taxon-dependent; these are not prospective discovery rates.

Skills and research value

Methods: R, XGBoost, maxnet / MaxEnt, brms Bayesian hierarchical modeling, geospatial cross-validation, ROC/PR metrics, model diagnostics, sensitivity analysis and GitHub Actions.

Practical takeaway: Match model choice to available predictors, spatial validation geometry and survey decisions—not a single leaderboard score. Follow-up work should test independent regions, harmonized model inputs and prospective survey costs.
