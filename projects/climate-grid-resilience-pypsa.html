Climate & Grid Resilience

A reproducible, four-balancing-authority screening study of how hypothetical heat and compound-weather stresses affect modeled power-system adequacy in the western United States.

View source code and methodology ↗Technical report ↗

Research scope

The study combines observed 2024 EIA-930 hourly demand and reported BA interchange, ERA5-based historical weather proxies, partial EIA-860 generator capacity data, and PyPSA/HiGHS optimization. The selected network includes CISO, AZPS, BPAT and SRP; it is not a calibrated model of the full Western Interconnection.

Results and evidence

Four sampled seven-day windows compare a baseline, a +4°C heat scenario and a compound stress scenario. Under the current screening assumptions, AZPS modeled summer unserved energy increases from approximately 22,168 MWh at baseline to 45,641 MWh with heat and 90,344 MWh under compound stress. These are simulated scenario outcomes, not historical blackouts or operational reliability forecasts.

Model sensitivity and limitations

Capacity and assumed interconnection limits strongly influence simulated shortage estimates. The four-node network links, thermal availability assumptions and weather-response functions are illustrative. Real BA service-territory polygons and rated physical transmission routes have not been validated; historical HIFLD polygon candidates contain geometric defects and are not established as 2024 coverage.

Interpretation: A reproducible climate-stress screening and data-integration demonstration, not an independently calibrated power-grid reliability assessment. The next research step is calibration and out-of-sample validation using consistent historical BA, generation, interconnection and availability data.
