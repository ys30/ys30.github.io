Green Space, PM2.5 & Mental Health

A longitudinal exposure-analysis workflow linking multi-wave health survey data with remotely sensed greenness and air pollution.

RoleStudy design · data integration · modeling
Scope5 survey waves
MethodsR · MODIS · longitudinal models
FocusDecision-relevant evidence
Case study

The question

A longitudinal exposure-analysis workflow linking multi-wave health survey data with remotely sensed greenness and air pollution.

Data

Harmonized CHARLS waves (2011/12–2020), MODIS MOD13A3 monthly 1-km NDVI, urban land-cover masks and PM2.5 exposure series.

Approach

The analysis harmonizes survey variables across waves, links environmental exposure by place and time, constructs lagged exposure windows, and evaluates specifications with fixed effects, temporal controls and robustness checks. Mental health is represented using the CESD outcome.

What this demonstrates

Current analyses are being used to test whether greenness associations are robust to lag structure, pollution exposure and model specification. Results are treated as specification-dependent rather than presented as a single causal estimate.

Why it matters

This work reflects my broader approach to environmental data science: connect domain knowledge with careful data engineering, choose methods that fit the scientific question, make assumptions visible, and translate analytical output into evidence that can support decisions.
