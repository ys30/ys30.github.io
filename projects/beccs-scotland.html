BECCS Spatial Siting in Scotland

A spatially explicit framework for evaluating where bioenergy with carbon capture and storage could be more feasible in Scotland.

RoleSpatial modeling & research
ScopeScotland · 1-km grid
MethodsGIS · raster analysis · MCDA
FocusDecision-relevant evidence
Case study

The question

A spatially explicit framework for evaluating where bioenergy with carbon capture and storage could be more feasible in Scotland.

Data

Scotland-wide 1-km spatial layers representing biomass resources, transport access, electricity infrastructure, land-use constraints and access to CO₂ storage.

Approach

The project integrates heterogeneous spatial criteria on a common grid and uses GIS-based multi-criteria analysis to compare siting conditions. The workflow makes assumptions and constraints explicit so spatial suitability can be interpreted rather than treated as a black-box score.

What this demonstrates

The analysis produces spatial evidence for comparing candidate areas and examining trade-offs among biomass supply, infrastructure access, land constraints and carbon-storage connectivity.

Why it matters

This work reflects my broader approach to environmental data science: connect domain knowledge with careful data engineering, choose methods that fit the scientific question, make assumptions visible, and translate analytical output into evidence that can support decisions.
