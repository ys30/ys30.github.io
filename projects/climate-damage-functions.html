Sector-Specific Climate Damage Functions

A six-GCM framework that translates climate hazards into sector-specific physical and economic damages while preserving model uncertainty and heterogeneous response mechanisms.

Road infrastructure

Under SSP585 in 2041–2050, median annual road losses reach ¥4.228 billion in Beijing, ¥3.655 billion in Tianjin and ¥23.003 billion in Hebei. The six-GCM ranges are especially wide in Hebei. A positive-part road specification reduces decade-mean damages by 22.8–50.0% relative to the former absolute-change formulation, with a mean reduction of 39.2%.

Concrete & labor

SSP585 median additional concrete losses in 2041–2050 are ¥3.237 billion in Beijing, ¥0.929 billion in Tianjin and ¥2.581 billion in Hebei. For labor, 2050 high-intensity workability losses are 9.63%, 12.28% and 9.72%, respectively, with high-intensity work consistently more exposed than medium- and low-intensity work.

Methodological contribution

Each of six GCM projections is propagated separately through nonlinear sector-specific damage functions before ensemble medians and ranges are summarized. This avoids hiding nonlinear responses behind climate-input averaging and makes uncertainty part of the result.

Why sector detail matters

Agriculture can show mixed gains and losses and non-monotonic scenario ordering, while roads, concrete and labor respond through different physical mechanisms. Keeping these functions separate exposes adaptation priorities that a single aggregate damage curve can hide.

From damage functions to the wider economy

The direct shocks from this framework feed into the companion Climate Change & Regional Economic Impacts study, where a recursive-dynamic CGE model traces impacts through production networks, prices, investment and labor.
