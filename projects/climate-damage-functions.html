Sector-Specific Climate Damage Functions

Six-GCM, multi-scenario damage functions that preserve sector-specific climate responses instead of collapsing regional risk into one aggregate curve.

SectorsAgriculture · grids · roads · concrete · labor

Road infrastructure

Under SSP585 in 2041–2050, median annual road losses reach ¥4.228 billion in Beijing, ¥3.655 billion in Tianjin and ¥23.003 billion in Hebei. The six-GCM ranges are wide, especially in Hebei. Updating the road function to a positive-part formulation reduces decade-mean damages by 22.8–50.0% relative to the former absolute-change specification, with a mean reduction of 39.2%.

Concrete & labor

Concrete shows a smoother response: SSP585 median additional losses in 2041–2050 are ¥3.237 billion in Beijing, ¥0.929 billion in Tianjin and ¥2.581 billion in Hebei. For labor, 2050 high-intensity workability losses under SSP585 are 9.63%, 12.28% and 9.72% respectively; high-intensity work is consistently more affected than medium- and low-intensity work.

Why sector detail matters

Agriculture can show mixed gains and losses and non-monotonic scenario ordering, while roads, concrete and labor follow different physical mechanisms and uncertainty structures. Keeping those functions separate exposes adaptation priorities that an aggregate damage curve can hide.

Current manuscript status

These are results from the current v25.13 Earth’s Future revision. They are presented here as manuscript findings, not as peer-reviewed final estimates.
