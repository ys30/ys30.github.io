Western US Energy Transition Modeling

Reproducible Western US power-system research: verified 2030–2050 NLR EFS demand-source engineering across 11 states, a separately labeled candidate spatial and fixed-hour alignment study, and official 2024 EIA-860 generator-level intake for 5,665 units. A separate 35-zone PyPSA/HiGHS optimization demonstrates solver behavior with synthetic inputs; the 11-state case has not been admitted as a calibrated dispatch model.

Latest source-backed engineering results — 11-state EFS and official generator inventory

The 2030, 2040 and 2050 NLR Electrification Futures Study (EFS) archive covers 11 Western states with 289,080 state-hour source records. The engineering study retains the existing seven-state allocated load records and adds four numerically audited candidate source-state-to-ReEDS-zone mappings for CA, NV, OR and WA. The resulting combined scenario uses real EFS source loads, but the four-state geography and physical UTC alignment remain candidate-only; it is not observed or model-solved Western generation.

New source-topology QA: A verified six-DC-Link audit finds three reciprocal directional DC corridors, none of which change the archive's 879 Line+Transformer connected components or 877 degree-zero buses under an undirected topology comparison. The follow-up 877-node classification identifies 157 offshore auxiliary nodes and 720 non-offshore nodes; 55 zero-degree records have positive original Pd, summing to 1,714.97 MW of source coefficients. This is not evidence of actual electricity outages or unmet demand. It instead reveals a model-source classification gap that should be addressed before network pruning and supply–demand coupling.

New EFS source/topology cross-check: Passing source-data audits find that 53 of the 55 original positive-Pd, graph-zero-degree low-voltage buses carry exported state labels within the 11-state EFS study (2 Texas buses are excluded). These numbers are not measured or simulated unserved energy, not authenticated final bus-level load, and not a completed Western OPF: the original upstream state denominator and utility bus-equipment types are still unverified.

Actual `Load.bus` check resolves an uncertainty: An end-to-end archived NetCDF audit shows that the 2030, 2040 and 2050 seven-state EFS load-only pilot networks each have 20 Load components (19 dynamic, one static-zero), and none attach to the 53 low-voltage graph-zero-degree source buses previously screened using hypothetical state×LAF coefficients. Every selected Load bus falls inside the largest Line+Transformer graph component. New numerical load-placement sensitivity: CI-verified original 2030 seven-state EFS data show that replacing the pilot's one first-indexed bus per zone assumption with a transparent multi-bus distribution proportional to original positive Pd among main-graph-connected candidate buses reallocates 99.61% of the modeled bus-level demand placement (273.648 TWh annually), but preserves all 8,760 hourly zonal demands and the original 274.707 TWh yearly demand exactly. Nineteen zones have positive-source-Pd connected candidates; the remaining zero-demand zone uses a documented fallback. This is a model spatial-assumption sensitivity, not physical electricity moved, unmet load, utility-certified delivery locations or an OPF result. Passing CI ↗

New source-coordinate validation of load-placement uncertainty: A real archived Western network audit compares the original first-bus and largest-source-Pd candidates for the 20 saved 2030 seven-state EFS zonal Loads. Source-coordinate Haversine separations have a 153.8 km median, a 233.0 km annual-demand-weighted mean, and a 505.7 km maximum (NM p31, candidate IDs 2040690 and 2080000). This quantifies uncertainty in choosing model bus locations, not physical movement of electricity, electrical transmission distance, power-flow impacts or certified utility delivery sites. Independent 2030 state-boundary check: Two successful GIS and source-distance audits flag one of 20 original EFS seven-state pilot zonal Load representative buses: Arizona p28 at bus 2040000 is not covered by its exported-state polygon in the independent Natural Earth 10m layer, whereas all 20 single-max-source-Pd candidates match their exported states. The flagged zone contributes 71.278 TWh of original 2030 pilot annual demand; this is not proven misallocated, lost or physically stranded energy. Separately, selecting alternative bus representatives changes candidate straight-line distance by more than 200 km in 7 of 20 zones, associated with 51.61% of original pilot annual demand. These are screening diagnostics; historical original full_state, ReEDS delivery boundaries, actual utility connections and OPF remain unverified.

Why the plant-to-grid crosswalk requires independent validation

A second CI-verified audit groups the 63 geographically distant plants by shared nearest synthetic bus. They collapse to 15 candidate bus IDs; 58 plants (5,118.3 MW) share an assigned candidate ID with at least one other distant plant. The three largest candidate clusters total 4,736.8 MW, or 81.2% of the long-distance candidate capacity. The largest, bus 2053003, groups 21 plants and 33 units (2,847.6 MW), with maximum candidate distance 225.8 km. This is a documented geographic crosswalk risk, not demonstrated power-flow concentration or a utility-certified physical connection.

Wind accounting: a useful research finding

The 2024 EIA-923 generation of 28 wind plants mapped to BPAT by EIA-860 totals 8,820,435.41 MWh; EIA-930 hourly BPAT wind totals 6,839,734 MWh. The descriptive difference is 1,980,701.41 MWh. The EIA-930A reporting-population audit found 20 of the 28 plant IDs represented in BPAT's Schedule 2 inventory and 8 absent, with the latter corresponding to 1,802,779 MWh of EIA-923 generation.

This provides evidence that plant/BA reporting populations may differ. It does not prove that those 1,802,779 MWh explain an equivalent portion of the gap: EIA-930A records generators, while this comparison is plant-level, and operational reporting boundaries may differ. See the official population audit and cross-source reconciliation.

What the battery scenario actually shows

A separate 8,784-hour PyPSA study compares a hypothetical 1-GW/4-GWh battery against matched no-battery scenarios. Modeled unmet demand falls from 73,373 to 51,021 MWh, a reduction of 22,352 MWh (30.5%). Across three treatments for 55 imputed renewable observations, these modeled dispatch results were unchanged under the study assumptions. The reported $315.99 million operating-objective difference contains $223.52 million in assumed shortage penalties, depends on a $10,000/MWh shortage penalty, and excludes battery capital expenditure and degradation. It is not verified project profitability or a real WECC reliability gain. Full scenario assumptions and numerical evidence.

PyPSA network-optimization capability

In addition to the 35-zone NARIS optimization, a separate synthetic two-bus PyPSA/HiGHS linear optimization tests congestion and dispatch: 25, 50 and 125 MW line ratings yield 25, 50 and 100 MW solved flows and 75, 50 and 0 MW of backup generation. This demonstrates solver behavior, not verified historical WECC branch flows or actual system savings. Review the solver evidence.

Limitations and next phase

These delivered components support a research-engineering portfolio, not a calibrated interconnected western U.S. power-system model. Verified 2024 WECC line topology, electrical impedance and thermal ratings, accurate BA operational accounting and physical historical validation remain outstanding. A NARIS source-backed zonal planning-interface network is now solved with synthetic loads, but a source-authenticated historical AC network is still outstanding. The 11-state demand comparison is now a source-backed candidate input with verified numerical conservation and independently corroborated border-bus geography, while its absolute UTC timing remains unverified. A source-authenticated 2024 generator-unit inventory is also available, but no generator has an independently verified electrical connection in this synthetic-topology network. Future work must validate actual generator POIs, hourly availability/cost assumptions, zone and branch compatibility and physical time alignment before empirical 2030–2050 dispatch or renewable/storage conclusions.
